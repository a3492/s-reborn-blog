The following is a fictional scenario for illustration.

Interview editor Bin paused after seeing the first captions. The interviewee's name was split into syllables, and the same brand was spelled differently across the screen. All captions were already present. The task was not to generate new captions but to **find where the same word was written differently**.

**In one sentence:** Generate a draft with automatic captions, standardize names and specialized terms with a correction table, and check audio and timing again.

## Correcting words and correcting playback timing are different tasks

Automatic captions convert speech into a text draft. Bin needed to check both **what the caption says** and **when it appears and disappears**. Even correct spelling can confuse viewers if it appears before speech begins or remains after a speaker change.

A spelling table avoids deciding the same name repeatedly. Replacing every similar sound, however, can accidentally change another person or product. Confirm spelling from approved material, and verify replacement locations against the audio.

## Collect misrecognized names in one table

Follow the [official CapCut guide](https://www.capcut.com/help/how-to-recognise-subtitles) to select the audio language and generate captions. Menus may differ by version. Obtain correct names from filming materials or the people who approved them.

| Find in captions | Confirmed spelling | Check against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chae Ha Yeon (split) | Chae Hayeon | Interview introduction |
| Nexlayer / Next Layer | NextLayer | Brand spelling sheet |
| Cl oud | Cloud | Surrounding speech |

Bin's 18 minutes to correct a short interview using a prepared spelling table are part of the fictional scenario, not a benchmark. Actual time depends on audio quality and speaker count.

## Replace in context and replay from the beginning

1. Locate incorrect phrases in the caption list. Preview search-and-replace if supported, or edit individual lines. Do not replace different values indiscriminately.
2. Replay corrected lines with original audio to check the first syllable and ending. Listen again around numbers, negations and speaker changes.
3. Play the full final video or preview. Check overlap with buttons or titles at the bottom and readable line breaks on phones.

At the end, Bin found another inconsistent brand spelling. Adding it to the correction table with its timecode made the word searchable in the next episode.

## Review a speaker transition in a fictional interview

Bin replayed the introduction. When the first speaker said "I'm Chae Hayeon," Bin corrected the name in one line. When the next speaker mentioned the brand, Bin listened to verify it and applied the approved spelling only there. Similar-sounding names were left alone.

Bin moved the playhead just before the speaker transition. If the introductory caption appeared before the first syllable, its start was moved later. If it remained as the second speaker began, its end was shortened. Moving only the start can cause overlap, so **replay the corrected line with its two immediate neighbors**.

Finally, preview the actual publishing size. Break long lines at natural meaning boundaries. Check numbers, negations and names with audio. When selecting clips, [review the middle frames of a draft](/en/blog/ai-tools-general/higgsfield-ai-creative-suite/) to check images and captions separately.
